How to practise English every day

Author and date to be added 2 minute read

On this page

  1. Start small and keep it regular
  2. Read and listen to things you enjoy
  3. Speak out loud, even on your own
  4. Write a few sentences each day
  5. Look back at your week

You do not need hours of free time to practise English. A few short habits, repeated often, keep the language part of your day. Here are simple ideas you can start today and adjust to your own level.

Key takeaways

  • Short, regular practice is easier to keep up than long, rare sessions.
  • Mix reading, listening, speaking and writing across the week.
  • Choose material you enjoy and that suits your level.
  • Look back once a week at what you have learned.

Start small and keep it regular

Pick a moment that is already part of your day, such as your journey to work, a coffee break or the time before bed. Ten or fifteen minutes is enough to begin. What matters is coming back to it every day.

Write your plan down so it is easy to remember, for example: “On weekdays, I read one short article on the bus.”

Read and listen to things you enjoy

Choose topics you already like, such as sport, cooking, travel, technology or music. When the subject interests you, it is easier to keep going, even when some words are new.

  • Read a short news story, a blog post or a few pages of a book.
  • Listen to a podcast, a song or a video with English subtitles.
  • Note down three new words or phrases each time.

Speak out loud, even on your own

You can practise speaking without a partner. Read a paragraph aloud, repeat a sentence after the speaker in a video, or describe what you are doing while you cook or tidy up.

If you can, record yourself for one minute and listen back. Over time, you will hear what you want to work on next.

Write a few sentences each day

Keep a simple English diary. Two or three sentences about your day are enough. Try to use one of the new words you noted while reading or listening.

Look back at your week

Once a week, read through your notes. Which words do you remember? Which ones do you need to see again? Choose a few to practise in the coming week, and change your plan if something is not working.

If you are not sure of your level, a placement test can help you choose material that is neither too easy nor too hard.
